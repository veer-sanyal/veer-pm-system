## The four targets, all at one market

- Family-owned, established 2020, Mumbai roots, family recipes reportedly in use since 1975. 14+ kulfi flavors (Alphonso mango, paan, malai, kesar pista).

- **Why they're the best first interview:** a family business with a real origin story, a physical location, AND a duct-taped online presence. Ask how a party order actually reaches them and what happens when two people call two different numbers.

## What to actually look for

Do not go in with a survey. Go in with one question and let them talk. But the pattern to watch for is already visible from the outside:

**Every one of these four has improvised a different stack, and none of them has one thing that runs it.** WordPress plus FoodBooking plus two phone numbers. Hand-written HTML plus Instagram. Wix plus WhatsApp plus a loyalty program. That fragmentation *is* the finding, and it is exactly the gap Maya fills in India.

Questions worth asking, in rough priority:
1. "How do people actually place an order with you?" (then listen for how many channels come out)
2. "Who handles the messages when you're standing here at the market?"
3. "How did you decide what to post this week, and does it do anything?"
4. "What made you build the website, and would you do it again?"
5. "What's the thing that eats the most time that isn't cooking?"

## Practical notes

- **Do not interview during the dinner rush.** Arrive at 4:00 for setup, or catch them 7:30–8:00 while they pack up. Buy something first, always.
- Lead with genuine interest in their business, not with a project. If asked, "I'm a student building software for small sellers and I'm trying to understand how you actually run this" is true and sufficient.
- Two or three good conversations beats six rushed ones.
- **Kulphi has a Redmond storefront with posted weekday hours (10–2, 6–8)**, so they are the one target reachable outside Wednesday.

## Regulatory context worth knowing (do not lecture them about it)

Washington's **Cottage Food Operation Permit** is the license that governs home-based food sellers: **$355 for two years**, kitchen inspection required, **$35,000 annual gross sales cap**, direct-to-consumer only (farmers markets, farm stands, events), **no refrigerated products, no wholesale, no mail order, no out-of-state sales**. WSDA Food Safety Program, cottagefoods@agr.wa.gov.

That cap and those channel restrictions are a sharp structural contrast with Trice's Indian sellers, and it is a legitimately interesting difference to report back to Naveen: **the US micro-seller is legally capped and channel-restricted in ways the Indian one is not.** It changes what an agent like Maya would even be allowed to do here.
