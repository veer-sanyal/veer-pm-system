The findings

Severity is about a prospective parent, not about tidiness. Critical means it stops someone joining or giving.

Critical

136 links on your own site lead nowhere, and 122 of them are your camps

2 hrs

We tested every link on all 43 pages: 469 of them, one at a time. Two clear patterns account for almost all the damage, and both are one-time fixes rather than 136 separate jobs.

1. Your entire camp facilities directory is dead. 122 links point at a reservation system at sagamorebsa.org/162/… that no longer exists. Every one returns a 404. That includes every campsite at both camps (Raven, Acorn, Hickory, Pioneer, Whispering Pines, Blue Racer Flats), both dining halls, every cabin and shelter, the swimming pool, the rifle, shotgun and archery ranges, the canoes, kayaks, mountain bikes and inner tubes, the chapel, the ATV course, and every photo gallery attached to them.

A unit leader trying to book a campsite, or a parent deciding whether your camp is worth it, hits a 404 on every single one. For a council whose camps are the product, this is the most expensive thing on the list.

2. Thirteen links are missing the /htdocs/wordpress/ part of the address and 404 for that reason alone. These are not obscure pages:

Link text on your siteWhere it points
Contact Us/contactus/
Ways to Give/how-to-give
Guide to Safe Scouting/health-and-safety/gss
Barriers to Abuse/health-and-safety/gss/gss01/#a
Youth Protection: Cub Scout, Scouts BSA, Venturing, Bullying Awareness/training/youth-protection/…
Infinite Pathways Archive/newsletter-archive/
Foundation News/foundation-news/

Two of those are worth pausing on. “Ways to Give” is a dead link on a donor page, which is money walking away. And every youth protection link 404s, which for a youth-serving organisation is the one category where a broken link is more than an inconvenience.

The fix

The thirteen are a find-and-replace: insert /htdocs/wordpress into each address. Perhaps twenty minutes. The 122 camp links come from one system that moved, so the real question for your web volunteer is where camp reservations live now, and then it is one template change rather than 122 edits.

How we counted, and why the number is not larger

Our first automated pass reported 196 broken links. It was wrong. Nine council pages came back as failures purely because we were requesting them too quickly, and they load perfectly when asked one at a time. A further 51 were third-party sites (scouting.org, Facebook, Scout Shop) that block automated requests, which is not evidence that the link is dead for a person. We re-tested every council-owned suspect sequentially, with retries and a normal browser identity, and 136 survived. Those are the ones above. The 51 third-party links are recorded as unverified rather than broken, because we could not honestly check them.

Critical

Your join page is a picture, so nothing can read it

20 min
sagamorebsa.org/htdocs/wordpress/join/

The main content of the page is a single image file. Everything written on that flyer, the invitation and the details and the reasons to sign up, exists only as pixels. This is the actual markup:

<img width="768" height="1024" src=".../Cub-Scouts-Rocks-768x1024.jpg" alt="" />

Google cannot read a word of it, which is much of why the best page on your site does not pull its weight in search. A parent using a screen reader gets nothing at all: both images on the page carry an empty alt, so the page is effectively blank to them.

The fix

Keep the flyer. Type its words onto the page underneath it as ordinary text, and write a sentence describing the photo into its alt attribute.

Critical

Nothing on the site links to your best page

10 min

The join page is the strongest thing you have: a clear button above the fold, a real photograph of real children that visibly includes a girl, a den-by-grade breakdown, and the line “Scouting is for the whole family! Boys, girls, moms, dads, step-parents, grandparents.”

We crawled 41 pages. Not one of them links to it. Not the navigation, not the mobile menu, not your own sitemap. The “Join Scouting” menu item skips it and sends people to the national site, which means you never learn who was interested and cannot follow up with anyone who hesitated.

The fix

Repoint the “Join Scouting” menu item at your own page. Let it be the place that then sends people to BeAScout, so the visit is yours first.

Critical

Nobody can find out what Scouting costs

1 hr

The membership fee appears on none of your 41 pages. What a parent does find, on the Cub Scouts page, is “$225 early bird price runs until June 1st” and “$250 normal price after June 2nd” for an optional day camp. They reasonably conclude that Cub Scouts costs $225.

sagamorebsa.org/htdocs/wordpress/cub-scouts/

This one finding turned up in all three places we looked, and the three together are the argument:

  • 1The price is on none of your 41 web pages.
  • 2When a parent goes looking, there is nothing on your site for a search engine to return, because the answer is not written down anywhere.
  • 3Your 6 August Facebook post about the fee update drew 19 comments, several asking plainly what the fee is, and none has an answer.

Parents cannot find the number on your site, cannot find it in search, so they ask you in public and hear nothing back.

The fix

One page titled “What Scouting Costs in Sagamore Council”, plus a line on the Cub Scouts page making clear that $225 is an optional camp. Most of the hour is confirming the current council fee internally. Atlanta Area Council words it well: pack dues “typically range from $40 to $85 annually”.

Branding

The old name is still in the places machines read

15 min

To be precise, because an earlier draft of this review overstated it: the largest text in your phone header reads “Scouting America”, and “Boy Scouts of America” appears only as small curved text on the patch. Your header is not shouting the old name at parents. It is simply untidy, with the patch crowding the wordmark and clipped by the screen edge.

The renaming issue is real, but it lives where people do not look:

  • 1The logo’s alt text is literally alt="Boy Scouts of America", which is what Google indexes and a screen reader reads aloud.
  • 2All 28 content pages carry “Trademark 2013 Boy Scouts of America” in the footer, one template edit.
  • 3Your Google Business Profile still reads “Sagamore Council – Boy Scouts of America”.
  • 4Your X account still uses the old round BSA avatar.

Scouting America’s own renaming guidance asked that council sites be updated no later than 8 February 2025. Since girls are roughly a fifth to a quarter of new members, this is worth more than tidiness.

Search

Search is not your problem, but the name you rank on is being retired

The good news first, because our first pass got this wrong and said you were invisible:

SearchWhere you appear
sagamore councilHomepage, #1

cub scouts lafayette indianaYour Cub Scouts page, #1

Search is not your problem. A parent who knows your name, or who searches for Scouting in Lafayette, finds you at the top. We are not going to dress that up as a weakness.

One thing in that table is worth noticing, though. The query you rank #2 on is “boy scouts lafayette indiana”, using the name you are moving away from. As parents increasingly search “scouting america” or simply “cub scouts near me”, the phrase your pages are strongest on is the one being retired. That is a concrete argument for putting “Scouting America” and your town names into your page titles, which currently contain neither.

Underneath that, the site gives search engines very little: 40 of 41 pages have no description, so Google writes your snippets for you. There are no preview tags, which is why links you post to Facebook appear as bare addresses with no image. No page title contains Kokomo, Lafayette, Logansport or Indiana. One sitemap is empty and the other was generated in May 2014.

Why there are no screenshots in this section

Search engines serve automated browsers a challenge page instead of results, and the computer we captured from is not in Indiana, so local results would be wrong even if it passed. These positions were read by hand. They are worth thirty seconds of your own checking from your own computer, and we would rather say that than show you a picture we could not honestly take.

Accessibility

Two thirds of your images are invisible to screen readers and to Google

Across the 41 pages we crawled, 166 of 248 images carry no alt text. Alt text is the sentence a screen reader speaks and the only thing a search engine can read inside a picture. On a site whose best page is a picture, this compounds.

Also worth knowing: 31 of the 41 pages still contain http:// links, which browsers increasingly flag, and the homepage contains no photographs of young people at all. Every image on it is a logo, an icon or an event graphic.

Where to start

Do not try to fix all 166. Write alt text for the images on the homepage, the join page and the Cub Scouts page. That is the path a prospective parent walks.

One address quirk worth knowing

It will confuse anyone you hand this to.

Your homepage answers at sagamorebsa.org, but every interior page is served from sagamorebsa.org/htdocs/wordpress/. So sagamorebsa.org/join/ is a hard 404, while sagamorebsa.org/htdocs/wordpress/join/ is your real join page. It is not urgent and moving off it is a project rather than a task, but it explains why addresses look the way they do.

How we checked

Method, and the three claims that carry no screenshot

We crawled 41 pages and tested every link on them by requesting it and reading the response code. Screenshots were captured at phone width and desktop width in a real browser at retina resolution on 7 August 2026, and every one was reviewed before use. Several first-pass findings did not survive that review and have been removed rather than softened, including a claim that your join button was broken.

Three claims deliberately have no picture. Search positions and the Google Business Profile could not be captured: search engines show automated browsers a challenge page, and our machine geolocates outside Indiana. The alt-text count and the mobile menu being built as a heading rather than a button live in the page’s code, so a screenshot would show a normal-looking page, which is exactly the problem. It is invisible to a sighted mouse user and total for everyone else.
